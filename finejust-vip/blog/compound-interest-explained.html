1. What Is Compound Interest?

Often referred to as the "Eighth Wonder of the World," compound interest is the mathematical process by which an initial investment earns interest, and that earned interest is subsequently reinvested to generate its own earnings in future periods.

Unlike linear growth where you add a fixed dollar amount each year, compound growth creates an exponential hockey-stick curve that accelerates dramatically over decades.

2. Simple Interest vs. Compound Interest

In simple interest, returns are paid out only on the starting principal. In compound interest, the principal base continually expands:

3. The Universal Compound Interest Formula

The standard mathematical equation for calculating compound future value is:

A = P × [ 1 + (r ÷ n) ](n × t)

Where:

  • A: Final accumulated balance (Principal + Interest).
  • P: Initial principal deposit.
  • r: Annual nominal interest rate (in decimal format, e.g. 7% = 0.07).
  • n: Number of compounding periods per year (1 for annual, 12 for monthly, 365 for daily).
  • t: Total investment duration in years.

4. Compounding Frequency: Annual, Monthly & Daily

The more frequently interest is calculated and reinvested, the higher the final yield:

  • Annual (n=1): Interest added once at year end.
  • Monthly (n=12): Standard for savings accounts, mortgages, and bank fixed deposits. Calculate monthly loan installments with our EMI Calculator.
  • Daily (n=365): Standard for high-yield savings accounts and credit card interest.

5. The Rule of 72: Doubling Your Money

The Rule of 72 is an invaluable mental arithmetic shortcut to estimate how long it takes an investment to double at a given compound return rate:

Years to Double ≈ 72 ÷ Annual Interest Rate (%)

For example, if your index fund averages an 8% annual return: 72 ÷ 8 = 9 Years. Your balance will double every 9 years!

6. APR vs. APY: What Every Investor Must Know

APR (Annual Percentage Rate) is the simple stated rate that excludes compounding. APY (Annual Percentage Yield) reflects the true effective annual interest earned including compounding.

A savings account advertising a 5.0% APR compounded daily actually yields an APY of 5.13%. Compute rate gains with our Percentage Calculator.

7. Common Mistakes That Sabotage Compounding

Compounding Saboteurs
  • Starting Late: Delaying investing by just 10 years in your 20s can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars in final retirement balance.
  • Carrying High-Interest Revolving Debt: Compounding works in reverse on credit cards, accelerating liabilities.
  • High Expense Ratios: An extra 1% annual mutual fund management fee can eat away over 25% of your final 30-year portfolio wealth.
