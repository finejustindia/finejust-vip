1. Introduction to Goods and Services Tax (GST)

Implemented on July 1, 2017, India's Goods and Services Tax (GST) replaced a complex web of cascading indirect taxes including Central Excise Duty, Service Tax, State VAT, Entry Tax, and Octroi under the banner of "One Nation, One Tax."

GST is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based tax levied on every value addition across the supply chain, from raw material procurement to end-consumer retail.

2. Types of GST: CGST, SGST, UTGST, and IGST

Under India's dual GST architecture, tax jurisdiction depends on the geographic location of the supplier and the recipient:

  • Central GST (CGST): Collected by the Central Government on intra-state supplies of goods and services (sales within the same state).
  • State GST (SGST): Collected by the State Government on intra-state supplies.
  • Union Territory GST (UTGST): Levied in place of SGST in Union Territories without legislatures (e.g. Andaman & Nicobar, Chandigarh, Ladakh).
  • Integrated GST (IGST): Levied on all inter-state supplies (across state borders) as well as imports. IGST is collected by the Centre and shared with the destination state.

3. Standard GST Rate Slabs in India

Products and services are classified under standardized HSN and SAC codes across five primary rate tiers:

Tax Slab CGST + SGST Split Representative Items
0% (Exempt) 0% + 0% Fresh milk, vegetables, unbranded grains, books
5% (Merit) 2.5% + 2.5% Packaged food items, medicines, domestic economy flights
12% (Standard 1) 6.0% + 6.0% Processed food, cell phones, business class flights
18% (Standard 2) 9.0% + 9.0% IT services, software SaaS, restaurants, financial services
28% (Luxury/Demerit) 14.0% + 14.0% Automobiles, luxury motorcycles, gaming, tobacco (+ cess)

4. Mathematical Formulas (Inclusive vs. Exclusive)

Understanding whether a quotation is tax-exclusive or tax-inclusive is essential for accurate accounting.

GST Exclusive (Adding GST):
GST Amount = (Net Amount × GST Rate) ÷ 100
Invoice Gross = Net Amount + GST Amount

GST Inclusive (Extracting GST from MRP):
Net Amount = Gross Total ÷ [ 1 + (GST Rate ÷ 100) ]
GST Amount = Gross Total - Net Amount

5. Practical Invoicing Calculation Examples

Example 1: B2B Software Invoice (18% Intrastate)

A web developer in Mumbai bills a client in Pune Rs. 50,000 for consulting services:

  1. Intrastate transaction: 18% splits into 9% CGST and 9% SGST.
  2. CGST Amount: Rs. 50,000 × 0.09 = Rs. 4,500.
  3. SGST Amount: Rs. 50,000 × 0.09 = Rs. 4,500.
  4. Total Invoice Amount: Rs. 50,000 + Rs. 9,000 = Rs. 59,000.

Example 2: Retail Consumer Purchase (Reverse Math)

A consumer buys an electronic gadget with an inclusive MRP tag of Rs. 11,800 (18% slab):

  1. Net Value: Rs. 11,800 ÷ 1.18 = Rs. 10,000.
  2. GST Paid: Rs. 11,800 - Rs. 10,000 = Rs. 1,800.

6. How Input Tax Credit (ITC) Works

Input Tax Credit eliminates tax cascading. If a manufacturing firm pays Rs. 1,800 in GST on raw materials and collects Rs. 2,500 in GST on finished goods sold, it pays only the net difference (Rs. 2,500 - Rs. 1,800 = Rs. 700) to the government.

7. Common GST Invoicing Mistakes

Frequent GST Errors to Avoid
  • Charging CGST + SGST on interstate transactions: Interstate transactions must always use IGST.
  • Incorrectly extracting tax using direct multiplication: Calculating 18% of Rs. 11,800 yields Rs. 2,124 (wrong), rather than the true reverse tax of Rs. 1,800.
  • Missing mandatory invoice details: Every GST invoice must show supplier GSTIN, invoice serial number, HSN/SAC code, and place of supply.

8. Pro Tips for Small Businesses & Freelancers

Cash Flow & Reconciliation Tip

Reconcile your purchase invoices against GSTR-2B monthly to ensure your vendors have filed returns, guaranteeing your Input Tax Credit is never blocked by the portal.
