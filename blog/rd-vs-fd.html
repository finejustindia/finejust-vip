RD vs FD: Recurring Deposit vs Fixed Deposit Comparison Guide

Comprehensive comparison of Recurring Deposit (RD) vs Fixed Deposit (FD). Compare interest returns, deposit flexibility, liquidity, and tax rules.

Key Takeaways
  • Lump-Sum vs Monthly Savings: FDs require upfront lump-sum capital, while RDs build wealth through disciplined monthly deposits from your salary.
  • Interest Rate Parity: Banks generally offer identical interest rates for both RDs and FDs of matching tenures.
  • Compounding Differences: In an FD, the entire principal compounds from Day 1; in an RD, each monthly installment compounds for its remaining tenure.

1. RD vs FD Overview & Fundamentals

Both Recurring Deposits (RD) and Fixed Deposits (FD) are premier capital-guaranteed savings instruments offered by banks. While both offer identical sovereign risk profiles and locked-in interest rates, their deposit mechanics cater to entirely different cash flow stages:

  • Fixed Deposit (FD): Best suited when you have an existing lump-sum corpus (e.g., annual bonus, asset sale) to lock in at once.
  • Recurring Deposit (RD): Best suited for salaried professionals building a corpus through regular monthly contributions from ongoing income.

3. How RD Compounding Mathematics Works

In a Recurring Deposit, the first installment compounds for the full $N$ quarters, the second installment compounds for $N-1$ quarters, down to the final installment which compounds for only 1 quarter. The total maturity corpus is computed by summing the quarterly compounding future values of each individual installment:

M = ∑ [ P × (1 + r / 4)^(4 × (n - k + 1) / 12) ]
Where $P$ is the monthly deposit, $r$ is annual rate, $n$ is total months, and $k$ is the installment index.

4. Return & Yield Comparison (Worked Example)

Consider committing a total capital of $12,000 over 1 Year (12 months) at an interest rate of 7.0% p.a.:

  • Fixed Deposit: Depositing $12,000 lump-sum upfront earns $862 in interest (Maturity: $12,862).
  • Recurring Deposit: Depositing $1,000 monthly earns $464 in interest (Maturity: $12,464).

The FD earns higher dollar interest because the full $12,000 was invested on Day 1, whereas the RD invested capital gradually over the year.

5. Which is Better for Your Financial Goal?

If you have liquid savings sitting in a low-interest checking account, an FD delivers higher net interest. If you are investing out of monthly salary cash flows without existing lump-sum reserves, an RD provides the ideal disciplined framework.
