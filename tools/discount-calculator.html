The Mathematics of Retail Discounts and Stacked Promotions

Navigating commercial retail sales, holiday promotions (such as Black Friday and Cyber Monday), and loyalty reward tiers requires a clear understanding of price markdown mathematics. Retailers frequently deploy compound promotional structures—such as "Take 30% Off, Plus an Extra 15% at Checkout"—which require multi-stage multiplication rather than simple addition.

How Stacked / Compound Discounts Work

When multiple percentage discounts apply sequentially, each subsequent markdown is evaluated against the intermediate discounted subtotal, not the original MSRP.

1. First Markdown Subtotal = Original Price × [ 1 - (Discount 1 ÷ 100) ]
2. Second Markdown Subtotal = Subtotal 1 × [ 1 - (Discount 2 ÷ 100) ]
3. Final Checkout Total = Subtotal 2 × [ 1 + (Sales Tax ÷ 100) ]
4. Total Savings = Original Price - Subtotal 2

Pro Tips for Smart Shopping

  • Check Pre-Tax vs Post-Tax Discounts: Manufacturer coupons are occasionally taxed on the pre-discount value, whereas retailer store discounts reduce taxable totals directly.
  • Evaluate Volume Multi-Buys: A "Buy 3 for $30" promo on $12 items equals a 16.7% discount, which may not be cost-effective if you only need a single item.
  • Mental Math Trick for 20% Off: Calculate 10% by shifting the decimal one place left, then double that figure ($85.00 → $8.50 × 2 = $17.00 saved).
