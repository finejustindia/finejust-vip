How to Calculate ROI: Simple vs Annualized Return on Investment

Master Return on Investment (ROI) formulas with real-world examples. Learn simple ROI, Annualized ROI (CAGR), and net financial profit metrics.

Key Takeaways

  • Time Sensitivity: Simple ROI ignores time horizon; a 50% ROI over 1 year is vastly superior to a 50% ROI over 10 years.
  • Annualized ROI (CAGR): Standardizes multi-year returns into a yearly compound growth rate for apples-to-apples asset comparison.

1. What is Return on Investment (ROI)?

Return on Investment (ROI) is the universal financial metric used to evaluate the efficiency and profitability of an investment or compare the relative performance of multiple capital allocation choices. By expressing net financial gain relative to the cost of capital, ROI enables direct comparisons across stocks, real estate, business ventures, and marketing campaigns.

2. Simple ROI Formula & Worked Examples

The standard simple ROI formula measures total percentage gain over the entire holding period:

ROI (%) = [(Final Value - Initial Investment) / Initial Investment] × 100

Example: If you invest $10,000 in a stock portfolio and sell it 3 years later for $16,500:

  • Net Profit: $16,500 - $10,000 = $6,500
  • Simple ROI: ($6,500 ÷ $10,000) × 100 = +65.0%

3. Annualized ROI (CAGR) Formula

The primary flaw of simple ROI is its inability to account for the duration of the investment. An asset earning +50% in 1 year represents exceptional performance, whereas +50% over 10 years represents a modest ~4.1% annualized yield. To solve this, financial analysts calculate the Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR):

Annualized ROI (CAGR) = [(Final Value / Initial Investment)^(1 / Years)] - 1

Using our earlier example ($10,000 growing to $16,500 over 3 years):

  • Annualized ROI: [($16,500 / $10,000)^(1 / 3)] - 1 = +18.17% per year

4. Real Estate, Marketing & Stock Market ROI

When calculating ROI in practical business contexts, remember to factor in secondary holding expenses:

  • Real Estate: Subtract property taxes, maintenance costs, mortgage interest, and closing fees from gross rental income.
  • Marketing (ROAS): Subtract agency retainers and creative production costs from gross revenue generated by ad spend.
  • Stock Investing: Factor in brokerage commissions and capital gains tax liabilities.
