How to Calculate Percentage: Formula, Steps & Examples

A beginner-friendly guide to mastering percentage formulas, price discounts, growth rates, retail markdowns, and fast mental calculation tricks.

  • Basic formula: Percentage = (Part ÷ Total) × 100.

1. What Is a Percentage?

A percentage is simply a ratio expressed as a fraction of 100. When we say 25%, we mean 25 parts out of 100 total parts, which simplifies to the fraction 1/4 or the decimal 0.25.

Percentages provide a standardized way to compare proportions across datasets of varying sizes. Comparing a score of 38 out of 45 with 72 out of 85 is difficult to evaluate at a glance. Converting both to percentages (84.4% vs 84.7%) gives immediate clarity.

From financial budgets and exam grades to sales promotions and interest rates, percentages form the universal language of numerical comparison in modern life.

2. The Core Percentage Formula (With Worked Examples)

Every percentage calculation revolves around three fundamental variables: the Base Total (the whole), the Part (the portion), and the Rate (the percentage). Depending on what you are solving for, the relationship is expressed as follows:

Finding Percentage Rate: Rate (%) = (Part ÷ Total) × 100
Finding the Part Value: Part = Total × (Rate ÷ 100)
Finding the Base Total: Total = Part ÷ (Rate ÷ 100)

Example 1: Finding the Percentage of a Total

Suppose an employee takes a certification exam with 80 total questions and scores 68 correct answers. What is their passing score percentage?

  1. Identify the Part: 68
  2. Identify the Total: 80
  3. Divide Part by Total: 68 ÷ 80 = 0.85
  4. Multiply by 100: 0.85 × 100 = 85%

Example 2: Finding the Part Value

A restaurant bill comes to $140, and you wish to leave an 18% tip. How much is the tip amount?

Convert 18% to a decimal (18 ÷ 100 = 0.18). Multiply by $140: 0.18 × $140 = $25.20. Your total bill with tip is $165.20.

3. Percentage Increase and Decrease

When tracking business revenue, stock returns, or weight loss, you must calculate the relative rate of change over time. The formula for percentage change is:

Percentage Change (%) = [ (New Value - Old Value) ÷ |Old Value| ] × 100%

If the result is positive, it signifies a Percentage Increase. If negative, it represents a Percentage Decrease.

Worked Example: Revenue Growth

A SaaS company grew its monthly recurring revenue from $40,000 in January to $54,000 in December. What was the annual growth rate?

  1. Calculate the absolute change: $54,000 - $40,000 = $14,000.
  2. Divide by original baseline: $14,000 ÷ $40,000 = 0.35.
  3. Multiply by 100: 0.35 × 100 = +35% Growth.

4. Calculating Discounts, Markups, and Sales Tax

Shopping discounts and commercial tax computations are everyday applications of percentages. Understanding the arithmetic helps avoid checkout surprises.

  • Discount Calculation: To find savings, multiply Original Price × (Discount % / 100). For final price, multiply Original Price × (1 - Discount % / 100). Check our Discount Calculator for stacked markdowns.
  • Tax Calculation: To compute sales tax or VAT, multiply Net Price × (Tax Rate / 100). For Indian Goods and Services Tax, use our dedicated GST Calculator.

5. Converting Fractions and Decimals to Percentages

Converting back and forth between fractions, decimals, and percentages is straightforward:

  • Decimal to Percentage: Shift the decimal point two positions to the right (e.g. 0.075 becomes 7.5%).
  • Percentage to Decimal: Shift the decimal point two positions to the left (e.g. 82.5% becomes 0.825).
  • Fraction to Percentage: Divide numerator by denominator, then multiply by 100 (e.g. 5/8 = 0.625 = 62.5%).

6. Fast Mental Math Tricks (The 10%, 5%, 1% Method)

You do not always need a digital calculator to compute numbers in daily life. Using benchmark division rules allows instantaneous mental calculations:

  • Finding 10%: Move the decimal point one position to the left. 10% of $86 is $8.60.
  • Finding 1%: Move the decimal point two positions to the left. 1% of $86 is $0.86.
  • Finding 5%: Halve the 10% value. Half of $8.60 is $4.30.
  • Finding 15%: Add 10% and 5% together ($8.60 + $4.30 = $12.90).
  • Finding 20%: Double the 10% value ($8.60 × 2 = $17.20).

7. Common Percentage Calculation Mistakes

Avoid These Common Pitfalls
  • Asymmetrical Reversibility: A 50% loss requires a 100% gain to break even. If $100 drops by 50% to $50, gaining 50% on $50 only brings you to $75, not $100.
  • Confusing Percentage Points with Percentage Growth: An interest rate increasing from 2% to 4% is a 2 percentage point increase, but a 100% relative surge in borrowing costs.
  • Adding Consecutive Percentages: A 20% discount followed by a 20% coupon does not equal 40% off. It equals 36% total discount because the second discount applies to the discounted price.

8. Pro Tips for Everyday Calculations

Helpful Pro Tip: The Commutative Rule of Percentages

x% of y is always equal to y% of x.

For instance, calculating 16% of 75 in your head is tricky. But by reversing the numbers, calculating 75% of 16 is effortless (three-quarters of 16 is 12). Both expressions yield exactly 12!
