1. Understanding BMR vs TDEE

Every physiological movementâ€”from cellular repair and heartbeat regulation to heavy resistance trainingâ€”requires caloric energy. To manage your body composition effectively, you must distinguish between two key metabolic metrics:

  • Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR): The baseline number of calories your body burns in 24 hours simply staying alive at complete rest.
  • Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE): The total energy burned including BMR, the thermic effect of food (TEF), non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT), and structured physical exercise.

2. The Mifflin-St Jeor BMR Equation

Validated by the American Dietetic Association as the gold standard in clinical nutrition, the Mifflin-St Jeor Equation computes BMR using gender, weight, height, and age:

For Men: BMR = (10 × Weight in kg) + (6.25 × Height in cm) - (5 × Age) + 5

For Women: BMR = (10 × Weight in kg) + (6.25 × Height in cm) - (5 × Age) - 161

3. Physical Activity Level (PAL) Multipliers

To calculate your actual daily maintenance calories (TDEE), multiply your BMR by your physical activity tier:

Activity Tier Multiplier Description
Sedentary 1.20 Desk job, little to no structured exercise
Light Activity 1.375 Light workouts or sports 1-3 days/week
Moderate Activity 1.55 Moderate exercise or sports 3-5 days/week
Very Active 1.725 Hard training or sports 6-7 days/week
Extremely Active 1.90 Physical labor job plus twice-daily workouts

4. Setting Calorie Deficits for Fat Loss & Surpluses for Muscle

  • Weight Loss (Fat Loss): Consume TDEE - 500 kcal/day for a steady, sustainable fat loss rate of ~1 lb (0.45 kg) per week without hormonal crash.
  • Weight Gain (Lean Bulking): Consume TDEE + 300 to 500 kcal/day combined with progressive resistance training to maximize muscle hypertrophy while minimizing fat gain.
