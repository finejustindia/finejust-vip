The Science and Mathematics of Thermal Scales

Temperature is an objective comparative measure of hot and cold, reflecting the average kinetic energy of vibrating atoms and molecules within a thermodynamic system. Unlike simple linear dimension measurements (like length or weight), converting between temperature scales requires both proportional slope scaling and reference offset adjustments.

Mathematical Equations Across Major Temperature Scales

• Celsius to Fahrenheit: °F = ( °C × 9/5 ) + 32
• Fahrenheit to Celsius: °C = ( °F - 32 ) × 5/9
• Celsius to Kelvin: K = °C + 273.15
• Kelvin to Celsius: °C = K - 273.15
• Fahrenheit to Kelvin: K = ( °F - 32 ) × 5/9 + 273.15
• Fahrenheit to Rankine: °R = °F + 459.67
