1. What is a Fixed Deposit (FD)?

A Fixed Deposit (FD)â€”also known as a Term Deposit or Certificate of Deposit (CD)â€”is a low-risk financial instrument offered by commercial banks and non-banking financial corporations (NBFCs). Investors deposit a lump-sum amount for a predetermined tenure (ranging from 7 days to 10 years) at a guaranteed, locked-in interest rate.

Unlike equity markets or mutual funds whose valuations fluctuate with economic cycles, a Fixed Deposit offers 100% principal protection and predictable maturity wealth.

2. Cumulative vs Non-Cumulative FDs

When booking a Fixed Deposit, you must choose between two distinct interest payout structures:

Feature Cumulative Fixed Deposit Non-Cumulative Fixed Deposit
Interest Payout Paid only at final maturity Paid monthly, quarterly, or annually
Compounding Effect Full quarterly compound interest growth No compounding (interest is withdrawn)
Ideal For Long-term wealth accumulation Retirees needing steady cash flow
Total Return (APY) Higher total yield at end of term Lower total return due to zero compounding

3. FD Interest & Compounding Formulas

For standard cumulative Fixed Deposits with quarterly compounding, maturity wealth is calculated using the compound interest formula:

A = P × (1 + r / n)^(n × t)
Where:
A = Maturity Amount ($)
P = Initial Principal Deposit ($)
r = Annual Interest Rate (decimal: 7.5% = 0.075)
n = Compounding frequency per year (Quarterly = 4)
t = Deposit tenure in years

4. Impact of Compounding Frequencies (Quarterly vs Annual)

Most commercial banks calculate FD interest on a quarterly compounding basis. Because interest earned in Quarter 1 is added to principal for Quarter 2, your effective Annual Percentage Yield (APY) exceeds the nominal headline rate.

For example, an FD with a nominal rate of 8.0% p.a. compounded quarterly generates an effective annual yield of 8.24%.

5. Senior Citizen FD Rates & Benefits

Banking regulations in most jurisdictions allow commercial banks to offer senior citizens (individuals aged 60+) an additional interest rate premiumâ€”typically +0.50% to +0.75% higher than standard consumer rates. Over a 5-year tenure on a $50,000 deposit, this extra rate adds thousands of dollars in risk-free interest income.

6. Tax Rules, TDS & Form 15G/15H

Interest earned on Fixed Deposits is classified as taxable income and added to your annual tax bracket. When interest income exceeds statutory annual thresholds, banks automatically deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS). Eligible individuals with zero total taxable liability can submit Form 15G (under 60) or Form 15H (senior citizens) to claim TDS exemptions.
